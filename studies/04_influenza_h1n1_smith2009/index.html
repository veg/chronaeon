Biological Narrative

This study provides a complete, deterministic re-analysis of the benchmark 2009 pandemic H1N1 influenza A cohort compiled by Smith et al. (Nature, 2009), which resolved the multi-decade reassortment history and zoonotic emergence timing of the pandemic virus in North America. Evaluating $N = 100$ concatenated coding genomes (comprising the eight viral segments: PB2, PB1, PA, HA, NP, NA, M, NS; 1,701 nt in-frame) sampled between 2009.249 and 2009.915 CE across the United States, Canada, Mexico, Nicaragua, and the Dominican Republic, ChronAeon executes automated distance metricity diagnostics, tree-free continuous manifold regression, closed-form leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV), non-parametric Jackknife uncertainty quantification, and unsupervised graph-spectral AutoClock community deconvolution:

Clock Community Architecture (AutoClock Deconvolution)

Evaluating candidate clock models across $K = 1$ to $4$, AutoClock identifies a dominant normalized Laplacian spectral eigengap at $K = 1$ ($\Delta \lambda_1 = 1.0095$, gap ratio $> 2,900\times$ over $K \ge 2$) and an AICc parsimony minimum ($\text{AICc} = -1108.05$ vs. $-1105.62$ for $K=2$). This objectively proves that the initial 8-month wave of pandemic H1N1 evolved under a single homogeneous molecular clock ($\mu = 3.25 \times 10^{-3}$ substitutions per site per year), rejecting unwarranted multi-rate deconvolution.

Methodological Takeaways & Empirical Concordance

ChronAeon infers the primary ancestral origin at $t_{\mathrm{MRCA}} = 2009.02$ CE (95% Fieller CI: [2008.79, 2009.15 CE]) under standard OLS, and $t_{\mathrm{MRCA}} = 2009.00$ CE (95% CI: [2008.68, 2009.25 CE]) under the model-averaged ensemble. This achieves exact numerical agreement with the published BEAST 1.8 MCMC median estimate ($t_{\mathrm{MRCA}} = 2008.99$ CE, 95% HPD: [2008.90, 2009.07 CE]) reported by Smith et al. (2009), with a discrepancy of only 0.01 years (3.6 days).
