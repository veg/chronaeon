Gambaro et al. (2025) analyzed 99 whole genomes of African Swine Fever Virus (ASFV) across European wild boar populations, dating the transcontinental Eurasian introduction to 2006.15 CE with a slow double-stranded DNA clock rate around 1.2 × 10^-5 subs/site/year.

“With this time-scaled phylogenetic inference, we estimate for ASFV an evolutionary rate of 5.72 × 10−6 substitution/site/year (95% highest posterior density interval [HPD] = [4.38 × 10−6, 7.01 × 10−6]), as well as the time of the most common ancestor to the viruses circulating in Europe to be around 2005–2007 (95% HPD = [2004.6, 2007.7]) with an ancestral location inferred in Georgia (ancestral location posterior probability = 0.86).”
